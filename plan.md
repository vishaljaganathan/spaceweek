## 1. Executive Summary

"Mission Mars: From Earth to the Red Planet" is an interactive, mission-based space science outreach programme designed for **50 students of Class XI (Science stream)**[cite: 1]. 

Hosted at the **Pondicherry Planetarium**, this event connects core science concepts into one continuous storyline[cite: 1]. By making full use of the planetarium's digital dome projector, students will visually explore the Moon, Mars, and the Solar System while participating in simple, manageable, and engaging hands-on activities[cite: 1].

---

## 2. Strategic Destination Choice: Why Mars Instead of the Moon's Other Side?

Before beginning our journey, we explore an important question: *Why are we choosing to go to Mars instead of the far side of the Moon?*

| Feature | The Moon's Far Side (Other Side) | Mars (The Red Planet) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Atmosphere** | No atmosphere (complete vacuum). | Has a thin atmosphere ($CO_2$), allowing parachute landing and weather studies[cite: 1]. |
| **Water & Life** | Frozen ice in deep dark craters; no chance of past life. | Signs of ancient river valleys, ice caps, and the search for past microscopic life[cite: 1]. |
| **Resources** | Rocks and dust (regolith). | Has carbon dioxide and water ice that can be converted into oxygen and rocket fuel. |
| **Communication** | Blocked directly by the Moon; needs relay satellites. | Direct communication possible, but with a real-time time delay (4 to 24 minutes)[cite: 1]. |
| **The Big Challenge** | A stepping stone to test lunar bases. | The ultimate destination for human exploration and living on another world[cite: 1]. |

* **The Takeaway:** The Moon’s far side is great for quiet astronomy, but **Mars offers the ultimate complete science mission**—atmosphere entry, surface exploration, finding water, and preparing for future life[cite: 1].

## 3. Planetarium Projector Integration

The planetarium dome projector will be actively used across the sessions:
* **Session 1:** 360-degree projection of the Solar System, showing the orbits of Earth and other planets[cite: 1].
* **Session 2:** High-definition projection of the night sky, distant stars, and light spectra[cite: 1].
* **Session 3:** Side-by-side giant dome projection comparing the craters on the Moon's far side with the canyons and volcanoes of Mars.
* **Session 5 & 6:** Immersive landing view and panoramic 360-degree surface views from Mars rovers[cite: 1].

## 4. Detailed Session Plans

---

### SESSION 1 — OUR HOME: EARTH AND THE SOLAR SYSTEM
* **Presenter 1:** Earth & Planetary Science[cite: 1]
* **Time:** 10:15 AM – 10:55 AM (40 Mins)[cite: 1]
* **Key Question:** *"How did our home become the planet we live on today?"*[cite: 1]

**A. Core Topics (Simple & Clear)**
* How the Solar System and Earth formed[cite: 1].
* The 8 planets: Inner rocky planets vs. Outer gas giants[cite: 1].
* What makes Earth special and suitable for life[cite: 1].
* Introduction to our neighbor: Mars[cite: 1].

**B. Planetarium Dome Visual**
* Full-dome projection of the Solar System with planets moving in their orbital paths[cite: 1].

**C. Hands-on & Demonstration**
* Scale model demonstration showing the actual relative sizes and distances of the planets from the Sun[cite: 1].

---

### SESSION 2 — HOW DO WE SEE SPACE?
* **Presenter 2:** Astronomy & Telescopes[cite: 1]
* **Time:** 10:55 AM – 11:35 AM (40 Mins)[cite: 1]
* **Key Question:** *"How can we learn about something that is millions or billions of kilometres away?"*[cite: 1]

**A. Core Topics (Simple & Clear)**
* What is a telescope and why do we need it?[cite: 1]
* How lenses and mirrors collect light (Refracting vs. Reflecting telescopes)[cite: 1].
* How light gives away secrets: Introduction to spectroscopy (reading the rainbow of light)[cite: 1].

**B. Planetarium Dome Visual**
* Projecting distant galaxies, nebulae, and showing how a telescope zooms into faint deep-sky objects.

**C. Hands-on & Demonstration**
* Displaying a working optical telescope[cite: 1].
* Simple diffraction grating activity: Students look through small grating sheets at different light sources to see emission lines and identify gases[cite: 1].

---

### SESSION 3 — CHOOSING OUR DESTINATION: WHY MARS?
* **Presenter 3:** Mission Planning & Planetary Science[cite: 1]
* **Time:** 11:50 AM – 12:30 PM (40 Mins)[cite: 1]
* **Key Question:** *"If we were going to Mars, where would we explore and why?"*[cite: 1]

**A. Core Topics (Simple & Clear)**
* Mars compared with Earth: Atmosphere, gravity, temperature, and water ice[cite: 1].
* Comparison: Why choose Mars instead of the far side of the Moon?
* Challenges of human exploration[cite: 1].
* Success of India’s Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan)[cite: 1].

**B. Planetarium Dome Visual**
* Dome projection comparing the rugged, cratered far side of the Moon with the red deserts, dried lakebeds, and giant mountains of Mars.

**C. Hands-on & Interactive Activity**
* **Mars Map Landing Site Selection:** Teams look at a visual Mars surface map and pick the best spot to land based on flat ground, sunlight, and presence of underground water ice[cite: 1].

---

### SESSION 4 — BUILDING OUR SPACECRAFT: THE ROCKET
* **Presenter 4:** Aerospace & Propulsion[cite: 1]
* **Time:** 12:30 PM – 1:10 PM (40 Mins)[cite: 1]
* **Key Question:** *"How do we make something leave Earth and travel into space?"*[cite: 1]

**A. Core Topics (Simple & Clear)**
* Action and Reaction (Newton's 3rd Law) and rocket thrust[cite: 1].
* Why rockets have stages and carry their own fuel and oxygen[cite: 1].
* How gravity and air resistance affect rocket flight[cite: 1].

**B. Hands-on & Practical Activity**
* **Paper / Air Rocket Flight Experiment:** Teams build streamlined paper rockets, launch them using a simple air/pneumatic launcher, adjust the fin shapes and balance, and re-launch to see how design changes improve flight distance and stability[cite: 1].

---

### SESSION 5 — SURVIVING THE LANDING: MARS LANDER
* **Presenter 5:** Entry, Descent & Landing[cite: 1]
* **Time:** 2:00 PM – 2:40 PM (40 Mins)[cite: 1]
* **Key Question:** *"Can our spacecraft reach Mars without destroying itself when it lands?"*[cite: 1]

**A. Core Topics (Simple & Clear)**
* Entering the Martian atmosphere at high speeds[cite: 1].
* Slowing down: Heat shields, parachutes, and landing thrusters[cite: 1].
* Why landing on Mars is tricky (thin air means parachutes alone are not enough)[cite: 1].

**B. Planetarium Dome Visual**
* First-person animation projected on the dome showing a spacecraft entering the Mars atmosphere and descending to the surface.

**C. Hands-on & Demonstration**
* **Lander Drop Challenge:** Teams design a safe landing capsule for a delicate payload (like a small ball/egg) using simple materials (cups, bubble wrap, small plastic parachutes) and test dropping it onto simulated rocky ground[cite: 1].

---

### SESSION 6 — EXPLORING MARS WITH A ROVER
* **Presenter 6:** Robotics & Exploration[cite: 1]
* **Time:** 2:40 PM – 3:20 PM (40 Mins)[cite: 1]
* **Key Question:** *"If humans cannot immediately go to Mars, how can we explore it?"*[cite: 1]

**A. Core Topics (Simple & Clear)**
* What is a planetary rover and why do we use wheels with special suspension?[cite: 1]
* Rover eyes and tools: Cameras, robotic arms, and obstacle sensors[cite: 1].
* How rovers make smart decisions using simple automated programming[cite: 1].

**B. Planetarium Dome Visual**
* 360-degree high-definition Martian surface panorama projected on the dome[cite: 1].

**C. Hands-on & Demonstration**
* Demonstration of a working small robotic rover (Arduino-based) moving across a simulated terrain, using sensors to detect and avoid rocks to reach a target[cite: 1].

---

### SESSION 7 — COMMUNICATING WITH EARTH
* **Presenter 7:** Space Communication & Mission Control[cite: 1]
* **Time:** 3:20 PM – 4:00 PM (40 Mins)[cite: 1]
* **Key Question:** *"How does a spacecraft millions of kilometres away tell us what it has discovered?"*[cite: 1]

**A. Core Topics (Simple & Clear)**
* How radio signals travel through space at the speed of light[cite: 1].
* Understanding time delay: Why a message to Mars takes several minutes to arrive[cite: 1].
* The basic path: Information $\rightarrow$ Encode $\rightarrow$ Transmit $\rightarrow$ Receive $\rightarrow$ Decode[cite: 1].

**B. Hands-on & Interactive Activity**
* **Mission Control Telemetry Game:** One student team acts as the "Rover" and another as "Earth Mission Control"[cite: 1]. The rover encodes simple discovery data (e.g., "Found Ice at Crater B") into binary/codes with an enforced time delay, and Mission Control must decode and verify the message[cite: 1].

---

### FINAL MISSION — MISSION COMPLETE
* **Lead Facilitators:** All 7 Presenters[cite: 1]
* **Time:** 4:00 PM – 4:15 PM (15 Mins)[cite: 1]

**A. Integrated Scenario Challenge**
* All 7 presenters present a combined emergency scenario to the students: A dust storm is blocking sunlight, power is low, and an obstacle is ahead[cite: 1].
* Students work in their teams to use what they learned in all 7 sessions (Power, Terrain, Navigation, and Communication) to solve the problem and safely send the final discovery data back to Earth[cite: 1].
